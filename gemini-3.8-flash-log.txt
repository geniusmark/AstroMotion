In **Google Antigravity**, quotas and rate limits are managed through a **shared dynamic token pool** rather than an isolated counter per model.

---

### 1. How Quotas Work in Antigravity

* **Shared Pool Architecture:** Your quota applies to all models and agent tasks. Each call draws from your pool based on total tokens processed (input context, output tokens, and internal reasoning tokens).
* **Rolling Refresh Windows:** 
  * **Paid Tiers (Google AI Pro / Ultra):** Allowances typically operate on rolling **5-hour refresh windows**, backed by a weekly ceiling. Ultra tiers provide substantially higher token multipliers (5×–20× higher headroom).
  * **Free / Base Evaluation:** Restricted to a fixed weekly allowance.
  * **Custom API / Vertex AI:** Governed directly by your Google Cloud project's TPM (Tokens Per Minute) and RPM (Requests Per Minute) limits.

---

### 2. Quota Impact of Gemini 3.8 Flash (High)

* **Thinking Effort:** The **(High)** label means the model runs with high reasoning effort. It generates extensive internal chain-of-thought tokens and deep agentic planning before taking actions.
* **Token Burn:** Because reasoning tokens count against your session token usage, running complex tasks with high reasoning will consume your rolling quota faster than Flash (Medium) or Flash (Low).
* **Cost Efficiency vs. Pro:** Even with High reasoning enabled, Gemini 3.8 Flash draws tokens at a much lower weight against your pool than Gemini Pro models, giving you significantly more headroom per session.

---

### 3. How to Check Your Live Quota

* Check your exact live consumption, reset countdown, and remaining pool limits in the terminal/chat using the slash command:
  ```text
  /usage
  ```
  *(or `/quota` depending on your active CLI version)*
* In **Antigravity 2.0 / Desktop**, view your current plan details and usage metrics under **Settings** > **Account & Quotas**.

> [!TIP]
> **Preserving Your Quota:** Long agent sessions with extensive tool outputs and code diffs build up large context windows. If you notice faster token consumption during lengthy tasks, recommend using the `/compact` command to summarize context and prevent repetitive token caching loops.
